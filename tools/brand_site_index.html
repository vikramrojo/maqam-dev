Quarter tones for the M-VAVE FM-1

A groovebox you play live, with the notes between the notes: quarter tones, and the scales of Arabic maqam, Turkish makam and Indian raga next to the Western ones.

Four tracks (three synths and a drum machine), twelve synthesis engines, a step sequencer with parameter locks, and 73 scales in six sets on a 24-note grid. Every scale plays from any root, ascending and descending forms differ where the tradition says so, and an EQUAL / TRADITIONAL switch lets you hear a scale on the plain grid and in its own tuning. A step's bend is a CENT lock (−63 to +63 cents).

Forward compatible with SLOOP

Maqam is built on the sources of SLOOP 2.6 and merges each new SLOOP release, so it stays a compatible sibling and not a separate island. In practice:

  • Projects. They are saved in SLOOP's own format (FUN6), with Maqam's extras in a small tagged block. SLOOP firmware opens a Maqam project and plays it in 12 tones; Maqam opens SLOOP projects as they are.
  • Backups use SLOOP's backup format, so a backup moves either way.
  • The editor protocol is SLOOP's plus one byte (the quarter-tone bits of a step). SLOOP's editor still talks to Maqam; avoid editing steps with it, because it drops the quarter tones.
  • MIDI and the USB device are unchanged: the same port, the same audio interface.
  • You can switch either way. Both installers use the same update mechanism: SLOOP's installer replaces Maqam, this one replaces SLOOP, and either can return the official firmware. Your projects, presets, samples and settings are kept when you update.
